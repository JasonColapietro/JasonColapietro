## Why the code comes with it

Most free audits are a trap and everybody in this industry knows it.

You get a 40-page PDF, auto-generated, mostly red. It tells you that you have 1,247 issues. It does not tell you which twelve matter. It is engineered to make you feel exactly one thing — *I cannot fix this myself* — and then the last page has a calendar link.

The withheld part is the point. The findings are the free sample; the remedy is the product. That's a legitimate business model and I'm choosing not to run it, because the thing I want is five good clients, not four hundred leads I have to nurture for a year.

## What we usually find

Not the 1,247 issues. The handful that move anything:

**Your site never states what you do in a form a machine can use.** Schema markup missing, wrong, or auto-generated by a plugin that's describing a blog post when the page is a service. A model reading your homepage has to infer your business from marketing copy, and it infers badly.

**Your entity is inconsistent.** Business name, address and service list differ across your site, your Google profile, your directory listings and your LinkedIn. Every inconsistency lowers the confidence a model has in any single fact about you, and low confidence means you get left out of the answer rather than stated wrong.

**You rank fine and still don't appear in answers.** This is the one that surprises people. Position 3 on a keyword you've owned for six years, and the AI assistant answering that same question names two competitors and not you. Ranking and being cited are now different mechanics.

**Your best content is unreachable.** Rendered client-side, buried four clicks deep, or sitting behind a "resources" gate. It's your strongest material and nothing can read it.

**Nobody has checked what the engines actually say.** Not the score — the sentences. Most owners have never once asked an assistant to describe their company and read the answer back. When you do, it's frequently outdated, occasionally wrong, sometimes just silent.

That last one is the whole reason the method leads with a screenshot rather than a score.

## SEO, AEO, GEO — the short version

**SEO** is the one you know: be in the list of links.

**AEO** is answer engine optimization: be named, correctly, when an assistant answers a question in your category.

**GEO** is the same problem across generative search surfaces.

They're separate terms because the old assumption broke. Classic SEO assumed a human sees ten links and picks one. Increasingly they see a synthesized paragraph naming two or three businesses. You're in the paragraph or you aren't, and a paragraph has no page two.

This is not a reason to abandon SEO. The technical foundation — crawlability, structure, speed, clean information architecture — is what makes you legible to both. It's a reason to stop measuring only the thing that used to be the whole game.
