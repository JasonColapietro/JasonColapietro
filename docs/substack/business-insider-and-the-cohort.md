## What the coverage was actually about

The cohort. Five businesses, working through SEO, AEO, and GEO together.

If those last two are new: **AEO** is answer engine optimization — showing up correctly when an AI assistant answers a question about you. **GEO** is generative engine optimization — the same problem across generative search surfaces. They exist as separate terms because the old assumptions stopped holding.

Classic SEO assumed a human would see a list of links and choose one. That human is increasingly not seeing a list. They're getting a synthesized paragraph that names two or three businesses and moves on. You are either in the paragraph or you are not, and there is no page two of a paragraph.

Here's the part that motivated the whole program: **most business owners have never seen what the AI engines say about them.** Not a guess about it. The actual text. They've checked their Google ranking for a decade and have never once asked ChatGPT, Gemini, or Google's Web Guide to describe their company and read the answer back.

When you do look, it's frequently wrong. Closed locations still listed. Old pricing. Services they stopped offering in 2023. A competitor named as the better option, in a sentence that reads with total confidence. Sometimes just silence — the engine doesn't mention them at all, because nothing on their site is structured in a way a model can use.

## Evidence first, then repair

That's why the method is built the way it is, and why the field guide is called [*The Screenshot*](https://seo.suedeai.ai/book).

[Suede Scan](https://scan.suedeai.ai) starts by asking the AI engines about your business and capturing what comes back — screenshots, dated, as the machines actually rendered them. Not a score out of 100. Not a traffic-light dashboard. The sentences, in the engine's own words.

Then the repair work: structured data, entity consistency, the machine-readable signals that let a model state a fact about you correctly instead of inferring one. [Suede Lens](https://suedeai.ai/lens), the Chrome extension, audits those signals on any page — rights, provenance, JSON-LD, SEO, AEO — and exports a portable Page Passport. Then you scan again and compare.

The reason to lead with evidence is that it's checkable in both directions. A screenshot of an engine getting you wrong in July, next to a screenshot of it getting you right in October, is an argument nobody has to trust me to follow. It also means I can't quietly move the goalposts, which is the point.

I apply the same rule to myself. On September 6, Google's Web Guide was asked to review Suede Labs AI and opened by calling the company "highly rated by users, earning a 5/5 rating from verified reviews." That's a nice sentence and I'll take it. It's also a **dated capture, not a standing ranking** — these surfaces are generated fresh and will say something different next quarter. It's on the [receipts page](https://seo.suedeai.ai/evidence) with its date attached, alongside everything else, including the things that are only true as of a specific day.

## Why five

Because five is how many I can do properly, alone.

I build solo. Every app, every site, every book, every upstream pull request under my name is my own work, and I'd rather say no to the sixth business than deliver five thin engagements and one apology. The cohort format exists for the same reason: five businesses working the same problem at the same time learn from each other's screenshots, and I write the playbook once instead of five times.

The operating history underneath this isn't marketing — it's two decades of call centers, where I learned that the only thing that survives contact with reality is a script somebody actually tested on a live call. The sales scripts I wrote in that niche are still in use across the industry. Same instinct here: test it, capture it, then claim it. That work also became Suede's [managed agents](https://seo.suedeai.ai/managed-ai-agents) for call review and lead qualification, which is a story for another post.

## What I'd tell you to do today, even if you never work with me

Open an AI assistant. Ask it: *"What does [your business name] do, and should I use them?"*

Read the answer all the way through. Screenshot it. Date the file.

That screenshot is your baseline, and you now have something almost nobody in your industry has: a dated record of what the machines currently say about you. Whatever you do next — hire me, hire someone else, fix it yourself over a long weekend — you'll be able to prove whether it worked.

Most people will not do this, because it's mildly unpleasant to find out. Do it anyway. The engines are already answering the question. The only variable is whether you've read the answer.
